## 8. Model / process notes

Built or fixed mid-playoffs — all now available for 2027:

- **Opposing-starter adjustment to win probability.** Facing an elite starter
  drops win rate from 37.3% to 26.7% (n=810 starter-games). Worth ~1.1 FP against
  a pitcher like Misiorowski.
- **Empirical QS/W rates** replacing a formula that gave 35% win probability to
  pitchers who had never completed six innings.
- **Park factors** (HR factor damped to a run factor).
- **Temperature/wind**, using the HR-Picks fitted coefficients (74°F baseline,
  +0.96%/°F, +0.97%/mph out to CF). Kansas City and Washington ran 1.23 HR
  environments on the final weekend.
- **Roof status + hourly precipitation checks** before any streaming add.
- **Handedness splits** pooled over a season-long window (a 5-week platoon split
  ran ~250 PA and produced a spurious 0.79 K multiplier that corrected to 0.93 on
  898 PA).

Known remaining gaps: opponent adjustment uses team aggregates rather than posted
lineups; walks are not opponent-adjusted; win probability ignores the pitcher's
own run support.

## 9. What to change for 2027

### Keep
1. **The streaming process. It is the best in the league and it isn't close.**
   70 moves, most innings, most strikeouts, most wins, most quality starts, and a
   better ERA than average. Do not scale this back.
2. Daily lineup management.

### Fix
1. **The draft is the single biggest fixable weakness — it cost 1,410 points
   against average.** The first six rounds are where it broke, and it was hitters.
   Raleigh, Seager, Springer and Stanton were all age/injury-risk profiles that
   broke. Weight durability and playing-time floor far more heavily early, because
   you cannot stream your way out of a dead first-rounder.
2. **Do not draft pitching early at all.** Glasnow (R7), Sheehan (R9),
   Strider (R10) and Bello (R18) were four busts and you still finished 1st in
   pitching points. Spend rounds 7–12 on bats.
3. **Reallocate the draft.** You took 10 pitchers and 9 hitters. Given you
   generate pitching from the wire at a +61% premium and hitting at +30%, go
   closer to **13–14 hitters / 5–6 pitchers**. That also creates the bench bats
   you lacked all year.
4. **Change the type of offense, not the amount.** You're 3rd in HR and 11th in
   singles, 9th in runs, 9th in steals. Target contact, on-base and speed. Steals
   are worth 2.0 each and you were 9th at 69, while two teams topped 105.
5. **Carry 1–2 bench hitters.** Week 19 Thursday you filled 4 of 9 hitter slots —
   about 25 points gone with no recourse.
6. **Consider a cheap saves source.** 3 saves all season cost ~164 points. Two
   competent closers is a top-3 overall scoring team.

### How to rank hitters

From a three-year stability test (~1,900 player-seasons):

| Metric | Y-o-Y stability | Predicts next-yr FP/G |
|---|---|---|
| HardHit% | **0.71** | 0.39 |
| ExitVelo | 0.69 | 0.42 |
| Barrel% | 0.68 | 0.38 |
| **FP/G** | 0.63 | **0.62** |
| Games | 0.57 | 0.37 |
| xwOBA | **0.37** | 0.50 |
| wOBA | **0.27** | 0.43 |

1. **Base = last season's FP/G.** Best single predictor.
2. **Adjust toward contact quality** (HardHit/ExitVelo/Barrel, which repeat at
   ~0.70) when results and process diverge.
3. **Skip xwOBA** — only 0.37 stable; it blends repeatable inputs with BABIP noise.
4. **Multiply by expected games** — prior games predicts at r ~ 0.44. A nudge,
   **not an injury filter**. Injuries are not forecastable.
5. **Then stop.** Inside the top 30-40 the ranking is worthless (rank
   correlation with outcome = **0.13**). Ceiling on any projection is
   **R^2 ~ 0.44**.
